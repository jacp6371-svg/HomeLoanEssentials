How it works

The assumptions

Every calculator simplifies. Here is exactly what this one assumes, so you know how far to trust the number.

  • The rate stays the same for the whole loan. In practice rates move.
  • Weekly and fortnightly repayments use the annual rate divided by 52 or 26. Some lenders calculate them differently.
  • Interest only: you pay interest only for the period chosen, then principal and interest over the remaining term.
  • Fees, offset accounts and extra repayments are not included.

Common questions

Questions people ask

Do fortnightly repayments save money?

Paying half your monthly repayment every fortnight means 26 half-payments a year, which is the same as 13 monthly payments. That extra month each year can take years off the loan. This calculator shows the exact fortnightly equivalent, so use the Amortisation calculator's extra repayment field to see that effect.

What is the difference between principal and interest and interest only?

Principal and interest repayments pay off the loan as well as the interest. Interest-only repayments are lower for a while but the loan does not go down, and repayments rise when the interest-only period ends.

Why do investors use interest only?

It can improve cash flow and suit some tax strategies. It costs more interest overall, so talk it through with Jack and your accountant.
