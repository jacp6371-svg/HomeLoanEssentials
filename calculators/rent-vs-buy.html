How it works

The assumptions

Every calculator simplifies. Here is exactly what this one assumes, so you know how far to trust the number.

  • Buying: your wealth is the property's value at the end, less the loan balance and selling costs.
  • Renting: you invest the deposit and purchase costs at the return you enter. Each year, whichever option costs less to live in invests the difference at the same return.
  • Stamp duty uses our Stamp Duty calculator's rates for an owner-occupier, with first home concessions if ticked (established home).
  • Tax, capital gains, Lenders Mortgage Insurance and changes in interest rates are not included.

Common questions

Questions people ask

Is it always better to buy than rent?

No. Over short periods, the upfront costs of buying (stamp duty, legal fees and selling costs) often outweigh any growth. Over longer periods buying tends to come out ahead, but it depends on prices, rents and rates.

What is the opportunity cost of a deposit?

Money tied up in a deposit cannot be invested elsewhere. If you rented, that money could earn a return, so the calculator counts it.
