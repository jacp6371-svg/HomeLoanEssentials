Fixed vs variable in 2026

Certainty or flexibility? The real trade-offs, and why a split loan often makes sense.

By Jack Perkins11 November 20266 min read

Fixed or variable is one of the most common questions we get, and there is no answer that is right for everyone. Nobody knows exactly where rates will go, including the banks. What you can control is choosing a structure that suits how you live and how much certainty you need.

Variable rates: flexibility

A variable rate moves up and down with the lender's pricing, which usually follows the Reserve Bank's cash rate.

  • You can usually make unlimited extra repayments and redraw them.
  • Offset accounts are common, so savings sitting in your account reduce the interest you pay.
  • You can refinance or sell without break costs.
  • The catch: if rates rise, so do your repayments.

Fixed rates: certainty

A fixed rate locks your rate for a set period, commonly one to five years.

  • Your repayments stay the same for the fixed period, which makes budgeting easier.
  • Extra repayments are often capped, and offset accounts are less common.
  • Breaking a fixed loan early can cost you. If you sell, refinance or pay it off during the fixed period, the lender may charge break costs, which can be significant.
  • The catch: if rates fall, you stay on the higher rate until the fixed term ends.

The middle ground: split loans

Many people split their loan, fixing part of it for certainty and leaving part variable for flexibility, extra repayments and an offset. It is not about guessing rates correctly. It is about not being caught out either way.

Questions to ask yourself

  1. Could my budget handle a rise in repayments right now?
  2. Am I likely to sell, refinance or make large extra repayments in the next few years?
  3. Do I have savings that would work harder in an offset account?
  4. How would I feel if I fixed and rates then fell, or stayed variable and rates rose?

What happens when a fixed rate ends

When the fixed period finishes, most loans roll onto the lender's standard variable rate, which is often higher than what new customers are offered. Put the date in your calendar and get your loan reviewed a couple of months before it expires.
